The Flow Game · Facilitator Guide

Run sheet for Anoop & the PgM

Hold this; don't project it and don't put it on the shared drive — it frames the discussion you'll lead.

This file lives outside the web app on purpose, so players can't open it. It contains no formulas or thresholds either — just how to run the room.

Before the session

  1. Players read the four books beforehand (The Goal, Phoenix, Unicorn, Rules of Flow) and the one-page player briefing.
  2. Start the server with a real password: cd server && CONWAY_ADMIN_PASSWORD='…' make server
  3. Sign in as admin → ⚙ Admin. Create one account per team; hand each table its username + password (shown once).
  4. Open /leaderboard.html on the projector and leave it up all session.
  5. Do one practice quarter yourselves so the controls feel familiar when questions come.

Run of show (~3 hours, 5 teams of 3–4)

TimeSegment
0:15Frame the goal & the rules. State plainly: the prize is the quality of your reasoning, not the highest number. Read the three subtle hints once.
×4 quarters~15 min plan/play → Resolve → scoreboard updates on the projector → ~15 min open discussion (questions below) → reveal the curve ball → next quarter.
0:60Final debrief: standings, the hidden-loop reveal, "which 3 moves do we adopt for real," anonymous pulse.

Between-round discussion — questions to tease out understanding

Ask 2–3 per round; rotate so every team speaks. After a team answers, ask the others: “did anyone make the opposite call?” The contrast is where the insight lands.

Reading the system / constraints — The Goal
• Where did you decide the system was most stuck this quarter, and what told you that?
• Did fixing one team's load move the bottleneck somewhere you didn't expect?
Flow & WIP — Rules of Flow, queueing
• Did anything you did speed the org up without anyone working harder? What was happening there?
• Where did you choose not to start work — and was that harder than choosing what to start?
Trade-offs & sequencing
• Which two of your moves reinforced each other? Which two fought each other?
• Was there a quarter you deliberately did less? What did that buy you?
• Did the story ever say a "good" move cost you something? What was the hidden bill?
People & sustainability
• Did a team's morale move on you? What were you trading to keep them whole — or what did you spend them on?
Ops / readiness / SRE — Phoenix & Unicorn
• When the incident or adoption shock hit, did it hurt more or less than you expected — and why?
• Did anything you shipped come back to bite you a quarter later?
Reflection & transfer — the real point
• What's one thing the simulation did that mirrors something real in our org right now?
• If you replayed this quarter, what would you change — and what concept changed your mind?

What to listen for (yours only)

  • Shaky understanding: "we just pulled every lever we could"; chasing the score number; treating a bad quarter as failure rather than signal; ignoring tired teams to chase output.
  • Strong understanding: naming one constraint and subordinating to it; doing less on purpose; pairing moves that reinforce; protecting people and readiness even when it costs this-quarter output; reading the after-action story and adjusting.
If a team is clearly optimizing the number instead of thinking, gently redirect: "Forget the score for a second — what did the org feel like to run this quarter?" The score is bait; the reasoning is the lesson.

Closing

Reveal the feedback loops they couldn't see, tied to each team's own choices. Then turn the room to reality: which moves from any playthrough do we actually make, and who owns them. End with an anonymous keep/kill/change on the game itself.
