Planning and execution guide

Understand the models, choose assumptions that match your work, and review delivery against an explicit agreement.

Plan a delivery periodPrepare inputs, choose assumptions and save an agreement. Review deliveryConnect evidence and decide what needs attention. Understand a metricChoose the right view and learn what its numbers mean. Learn the conceptsStart with the vocabulary and ideas behind Conway.

1. What each part of Conway answers

Choose a view by the decision you need to make
AreaQuestionInputs and output
PlanWhat could we deliver with this scope and capacity?Your roster, estimates, dependencies and scheduling policy produce a deterministic schedule. Repeating the same inputs gives the same forecast; it does not establish certainty about delivery.
Review execution, inside PlanWhat does captured delivery evidence say about our agreement?Confirmed epic bindings, a selected snapshot and the active agreement produce progress proxies, inferred dates and variance.
MeasureWhere does recorded work wait, and what might improve flow?A dated Jira or example snapshot drives the network, WIP, quality and simulation views. Refreshing a view does not capture new Jira activity.
LearnWhat happens when we try different management decisions?A separate game models consequences over simulated rounds. Scores are learning feedback, not a measurement of your real organization.

Use the models together, but keep their units and sources separate. Plan weeks, Jira item counts, simulation percentiles and game scores are not interchangeable. Start by naming the business outcome; a fuller chart or a lower utilization number is not itself the outcome.

3. The ideas behind the tool

Conway takes inspiration from the books below. The connections to controls are our application of those ideas. The implementation uses its own assumptions and heuristics; it is not a complete implementation of every method in these books.

BookManagement ideaApply it in Conway
The Goal
Eliyahu M. Goldratt and Jeff Cox		Understand the system's constraint and improve the flow of outcomes through the whole system.Inspect the drum and its dependent work before adding capacity. Compare portfolio consequences, not how busy one team looks.
Critical Chain
Eliyahu M. Goldratt		Manage project dependencies, resource contention and uncertainty at the chain level.Review the sequence, shared team tracks and protective buffer. Conway's percentage buffer and dispatch rules are modeling choices, not a guarantee or a full critical-chain implementation.
Goldratt's Rules of Flow
Efrat Goldratt-Ashlag		Improve delivery through triage, less harmful multitasking and preparation before starting.Choose an intentional WIP policy, check full kit, and defer work deliberately rather than starting everything to appear busy.
The Phoenix Project
Gene Kim, Kevin Behr and George Spafford		Make operational work and constraints visible; improve feedback and learning across delivery and operations.Allow for support and interruptions in capacity loss. Include operational readiness, review evidence regularly and record the next improvement action.
The Unicorn Project
Gene Kim		Improve locality, simplicity and the conditions in which teams deliver customer value.Use the dependency network to discuss ownership and interfaces. Explore fewer handoffs, protect improvement work and discuss model disagreements without blaming teams.

Practical habit: identify what limits the outcome, protect that capacity, align upstream releases with it, evaluate a change, and repeat when the constraint changes. An idle track can be the right result when starting more work would overload a shared dependency.

The formulas below describe Conway. For example, the default 25% buffer, the WIP load denominator and queue multipliers are not presented as formulas prescribed by these books. Publisher links open in a new tab for optional further reading; the guide itself is available offline.

4. Tutorial: create and agree a first plan

Use synthetic data first. Sign in with manager access, then allow about fifteen minutes to learn the workflow. Complete one step before moving on.

  1. Open Plan → My plans → Load demo plan. Confirm which plan is open. A populated plan opens at Plan commitments, also called Order.
  2. Inspect Plan setup. Review the roster and initiative list. For your own data, attach a roster before previewing the initiatives workbook. Check team names and every unestimated assignment.
  3. Open Assumptions. Set the period start, choose the estimate model that matches the spreadsheet and deliberately choose a WIP model. Use the situation table below rather than changing settings only to obtain a greener result.
  4. Read commitments and coverage together. Inspect target dates at risk, work that does not start, and work that finishes outside the period. A low lateness cost can coexist with omitted work.
  5. Open Timeline and group by team. Select an initiative, inspect the assigned slices and read the explanation for unplaced work. Empty tracks are not proof that all waiting work is eligible to start.
  6. Create a named scenario. Try one change: capacity, scope or ordering. Review affected initiatives. Applied edits save to the working plan, so use a copy for experiments you want to keep separate.
  7. Prepare execution associations. If you will review Jira evidence, open Review execution, choose its Execution snapshot and confirm epic bindings before saving the initial agreement. You can inspect binding coverage before an agreement exists.
  8. Agree the inputs and dates. Return to Plan commitments, open the agreement chip/drawer, enter a name and select Save current order. Confirm which agreement is active. This records the plan and bound scope you accepted; it does not update Jira.
  9. Review execution against that agreement. Recheck coverage before interpreting dates, then record an action and review date. If you first add bindings after agreeing, inspect the scope change and deliberately agree a replacement before using combined-scope variance.
Expected result: a named working plan, explicit assumptions, a saved agreement and a known evidence source. Forecast accuracy depends on inputs and the current model limitations.

5. Choose settings for your situation

These are starting recommendations for a discussion, not automatic best settings. Preserve the meaning of your input estimates and test alternatives in a scenario.

6. How to run planning and weekly review

Before agreeing the period

  1. Confirm the outcome, scope and priority with stakeholders.
  2. Validate the import, readiness and capacity assumptions with teams.
  3. Compare the current order and proposed alternatives. Review unstarted work before lateness, and inspect who loses capacity when another initiative moves earlier.
  4. Record the chosen policy and save an agreement only after accepting the tradeoffs.

At each execution review

  1. Confirm the plan, active agreement, snapshot capture date and project scope.
  2. Check bound versus tracked initiatives and missing evidence. A tracked initiative can still be only partially represented.
  3. Review scope changes, blockers, inferred variance and conditional remaining duration with the responsible teams.
  4. Record the next action, owner, review date and evidence. The decision log does not send reminders or update Jira.
  5. If the agreement must change, explore a scenario, discuss the consequences, then deliberately save a new agreement.

Refresh saved evidence rereads the selected snapshot. For newer observations, use Measure → Import from Jira, then explicitly select the new capture in Review execution. Do not replace the agreement merely to make variance disappear.

Managers select a saved roster, project scope and snapshot name in Measure → Import from Jira. The roster is required by the current UI. Choose how to count WIP:

  • Every in-progress story/task: counts in-progress non-epic issues, including subtasks when present. Use it for item-level queues.

9. Capacity, effort and duration calculations

Plan schedules on a whole-week grid. Fractions of required duration round up. The following examples assume available tracks, no dependencies, calendars, WIP holds, start pins or splitting overhead unless stated.

Effective tracks and capacity

An explicit positive track count takes precedence. Otherwise, pairing uses ceil(developers / 2); without pairing, the developer count is the track count. A team with no usable tracks has no scheduling capacity.

available track-weeks = tracks × horizon weeks × (1 − capacity loss)
Plan utilization = estimated demand track-weeks / available track-weeks

Example: 6 developers pairing give 3 tracks. Over 10 weeks with 20% loss, nominal capacity is 3 × 10 × 0.8 = 24 track-weeks. Demand of 30 gives utilization 30 / 24 = 1.25. This period-total ratio does not prove when capacity is available; the scheduler also checks week-by-week constraints.

A positive per-team loss overrides global loss. Blank or zero at team level currently inherits the global value, so zero cannot express a zero-loss exception to a positive global loss. Set recurring allowances deliberately; use calendars for dated interruptions.

Estimate model

ChoiceOrdinary duration calculationChoose when
Effortceil(remaining effort / (selected lanes × (1 − loss)))The estimate is total work and can be shared across lanes. Selected lanes may be fewer than the team's physical tracks.
Wall-clockceil(remaining estimate / (1 − loss)), normally on one lane.The estimate describes one work-stream's duration. If it already includes leave/support allowances, review loss to avoid counting them twice. Optional splitting can change placement.

Example: 12 track-weeks on 3 selected lanes with 20% loss take ceil(12 / 2.4) = 5 weeks in effort mode. Entering 12 in wall-clock mode takes ceil(12 / 0.8) = 15 weeks. The difference is the interpretation of the input, not an optimization win.

For in-flight work, the model reduces remaining estimates using entered progress: remaining = estimate × (1 − progress). That progress is a planning input; it is not automatically replaced by Jira issue-count progress.

Raw finish, buffer and commitment

chain duration = raw finish − initiative start
buffer weeks = ceil(chain duration × buffer fraction)
commitment week = raw finish + buffer weeks

Example: a chain starting at week 0 and finishing at week 5, with a 25% buffer, gets ceil(5 × 0.25) = 2 buffer weeks and a commitment at week 7. A zero buffer commits at week 5. A larger buffer changes the protected date; it does not create team capacity or increase measured confidence.

Dependencies, competing work and non-working intervals can lengthen the chain. The finite scheduler currently adds no timezone handoff delay. Team work finishes at its raw finish; the buffer is protective time, not extra scheduled effort on every track.

Open Assumptions from Plan commitments. Save one deliberate change and inspect its consequences. Existing plans retain their stored policy; an unchosen WIP model is evaluated as strict, and an unspecified estimate model is wall-clock. Do not assume suggested setup defaults are already saved.

Named lead capacity: advisory or hard

The default thresholds per named lead and role are PM 2, engineering 2, architecture 3 and program management 4 concurrent initiatives. Counts cover initiative start through raw finish, not just that person's actively worked weeks.

  • Advisory: schedule eligible team work and report overload. Use it while ownership and workload estimates are incomplete.
  • Hard: delay new releases at the role's limit. Use it only when named lead availability is a real release constraint.
  • Blank threshold: restores the role default. Zero: is a warning threshold in advisory mode and prevents new releases requiring that role in hard mode.

With no explicit role limits, an unspecified lead mode is advisory. Existing plans with explicit role limits default to hard until changed. Missing-owner placeholders are not treated as a single shared person. Existing in-flight work is not retroactively unstarted by these release gates.

Splitting and lane chunking

Splitting tax enables an optional placement that grows across available lanes when this is beneficial. Blank/zero disables that optional splitting. It does not turn effort mode into single-lane work: ordinary effort placement can already use several lanes.

The split placement charges the configured tax at the start and again whenever it grows to more lanes. During taxed ramp weeks it occupies lanes but consumes no effort. Choose a tax supported by experience with coordination and setup, then compare with splitting disabled.

With positive splitting tax and a positive chunk size, lane selection uses approximately ceil(remaining effort / chunk size), bounded by available team lanes. Work is then shared evenly across the selected lanes. It is not a hard maximum size per track when capacity is insufficient, and it does not create literal uneven chunks.

Example: 45 track-weeks, chunk size 20 and three available lanes select three lanes, then share the work as 15 each before loss and overhead. With only two available lanes, their shares exceed 20. With splitting tax disabled, the chunk threshold is inactive.

11. Sites and calendar windows

Plan's Sites & capacity editor records locations, IANA timezones and work hours. The site-overlap utility can calculate shared working hours for a modeled date, including daylight-saving offsets. Review ambiguous inferred zones and missing information.

Current scheduling limit: the finite scheduler adds zero handoff weeks between team slices. Entering timezones does not currently lengthen its commitments for cross-site coordination. Represent material coordination work explicitly and discuss the allowance; do not assume the timeline has priced it. Measure uses separate overlap proxies and handoff assumptions.

Calendar effectWhat happensExample use
Block startsBlocks new starts in the covered interval; it does not stop work already running.A release freeze or a period when onboarding a new initiative is impractical.
Block completionsMoves a modeled completion to a week in which completion is allowed.A deployment or acceptance freeze where work may continue but cannot complete.
Reduce capacityCurrently sets the scoped team's tracks to zero during the covered weeks; partial percentage reductions are not supported by this window.A holiday or shutdown. Use normal capacity-loss settings for a recurring partial reduction.

Check the window's scope: organization, site or team. Both endpoint weeks are included. Dates are mapped onto the weekly grid, so even a short event can affect its containing week. Inspect the resulting bands. A start freeze and a zero-capacity holiday answer different questions.

Measure differs: imported snapshots currently use coarse overlap defaults: eight hours for self, six for teams sharing a nonempty location, two otherwise. The simulator uses its own daily handoff bands. Setting a Plan timezone does not automatically replace snapshot overlap assumptions.

Calendar windows are clipped to the planning horizon; windows entirely outside it are ignored. A finish beyond that horizon may therefore omit later calendar constraints. Extend the modeled period and review those constraints when evaluating a later finish.

12. Plan commitments: ordering and optimization

The Order view shows the schedule under the working order. Optimize order compares dispatch rules and proposes a different order; accepting the proposal is explicit. A dispatch rule is a heuristic, not proof of a globally optimal schedule.

How alternatives are scored

Compare weighted unstarted work first, then weighted weeks late. These are separate costs. An initiative with no placement does not get a fabricated delivery date for scoring. A partial schedule with zero lateness can therefore be worse than a fuller schedule with some lateness.

weight = positive cost of delay (or 1) × tier multiplier × priority multiplier
tier multiplier: T1 = 4, T2 = 3, T3 = 2, T4/unset = 1
priority multiplier = 1 + 1/entered stated priority; unset = 1
dated lateness cost = weight × max(0, commitment − target)

Date-locked initiatives receive a further 100-fold weight increase in scheduling evaluation. These are relative portfolio costs, not a currency amount unless inputs and the intended interpretation support that. The model does not charge all real-world multitasking costs; do not choose WIP off solely because its dates look better.

Example: cost of delay 10, tier 2 and priority 1 produce weight 10 × 3 × 2 = 60. Two weeks late cost 120 before a date-lock multiplier. An unstarted initiative of the same weight contributes 60 to the separate unstarted-work cost.

14. Plan Dependencies and what-if levers

This network uses the open plan's team estimates and dependency cells. Org Network under Measure uses a selected snapshot. Their numbers may differ because their source and model differ.

Plan's dependency view first computes period-total demand/capacity. Its directional lead-time calculation multiplies each team's estimate by 1 / (1 − utilization), with utilization bounded to 0–0.95, then follows the longest dependent chain. At utilization 0.8 the multiplier is 5; at 0.95 it is 20. This is a sensitivity model, not the finite-track schedule or a promised date.

LeverModel changeDecision to confirm
Add capacityAdds explicit tracks to the selected team.Can the extra capacity really do this work, and when will it be effective?
UnpairDoubles the current effective track count and clears pairing, including when tracks were explicitly overridden.What happens to quality, learning and coordination? The capacity arithmetic does not settle that tradeoff.
Descope / drop teamReduces estimates or removes a team's contribution.Is the removed scope genuinely optional? Check dependencies and readiness.
DeferRemoves an initiative from the current scenario's demand.Who accepts the deferred outcome, and when will it be reconsidered?
ReassignMoves work between teams and combines affected inputs.Can the destination own it? Unknown estimates currently need special reconciliation after merging.
Reduce WIPThe directional dependency model treats it as assumed recovered multitasking waste and reduces demand. The finite scheduler uses release limits instead.Do not treat this assumed gain as measured productivity or count it again through lower estimates.

15. Read and edit the Gantt timeline

By initiative explains an outcome's team sequence. By pod/team explains track occupancy and contention. Both display the server schedule. Use a period start for calendar labels; otherwise weeks are relative.

  • Read filled bars as placed work and buffer tails as protective time. Phase gaps and track changes matter when work is split.
  • Inspect unscheduled/outside-period assignments as well as visible bars. A missing bar can mean a release hold or an unknown estimate.
  • Initiative search requires matching text or supported word endings, so rotate can match rotation. Team search retains shorthand matching. The other-work toggle can intentionally retain context bars that do not match the initiative query.
  • Use the team inspector to reconcile idle time with dependencies, readiness, lead policy, freezes and lane reservations.

Editing safely

Select a bar for precise controls. Moving supported work changes a start pin or lane reservation. Dragging an eligible estimate edge changes the estimate. Split/display-split and in-flight bars do not support that same edge-resize contract; their edges may act as moves. Use the inspector for an explicit edit rather than assuming every edge is a resize handle.

Applied changes autosave to the working plan. Wait for save feedback, then inspect the consequences. 17. Review execution with dated evidence

Open the plan's Review execution tab. It compares the active agreement with the selected Execution snapshot. Selecting a Measure snapshot does not automatically associate every initiative with its Jira work.

  1. Confirm the active agreement and its period start.
  2. Select an accessible execution snapshot and check its capture date.

  4. Check bound and tracked coverage, missing epic keys, unmapped teams and changed scope before interpreting dates or percentages.

Worked example: progress and remaining time

An agreed team slice starts at week 2 and lasts 4 weeks. At capture week 5, 3 of its 4 captured children are done. 20. Measure: Org Network

Use Org Network to investigate recorded cross-team dependencies in the selected snapshot. A directed edge means the from team blocks the to team; its count is the number of recorded links. Missing links in Jira mean missing edges here. An apparently independent team may simply have incomplete linkage.

Select a team to inspect its load and relationships. The comparison picker shows changes against another accessible snapshot. For a meaningful comparison, keep the project scope, roster, import WIP mode and collection method consistent; a changed import boundary can look like an organizational improvement.

Load and the constraint ranking

load = WIP / max(1, 2 × work streams)
modeled utilization = clamp(load, 0.05, 0.97)
queue factor = utilization / (1 − utilization)
constraint score = queue factor × (1 + distinct downstream dependents)

Work streams come from the roster override or derived headcount/pairing. Load is a concurrency proxy, not measured utilization. At utilization 0.8 the queue factor is 4; with three distinct dependents the score is 16. This suggests where to investigate. Confirm delays and real blocking work before declaring a system constraint.

Organization what-if controls

Use the network's organization simulation to explore structural alternatives. Merge/transfer scenarios combine team WIP and dependent edges, remove internal links and alter capacity, throughput and fitted statistics with simplifying weights. Some scenarios use different headcount-based load assumptions. A lower modeled queue is a hypothesis about organization design; it does not show that people can instantly absorb skills, scope or coordination responsibilities. Keep the original snapshot as the observation and record the proposed real-world experiment separately.

Org Flow Index and merge assumptions

coordination tax = sum(link count × handoff delay) + residual tax
source-team wait = min(WIP / (weekly throughput / 5), 180 days)
queue tax = sum(link count × source-team wait)
index = 50 × coordination tax / baseline coordination tax + 50 × queue tax / baseline queue tax

Missing positive throughput substitutes 30 days of wait. Denominators have a tiny positive floor; the baseline is 100 only when both components are positive. Lower means less modeled cost relative to that baseline. These weighted quantities are comparison heuristics, not an organizational delivery duration. Check that WIP and throughput use comparable item units.

In a merge with people-transfer fraction f, all scope and WIP move, but developers become A + round(f × B) and throughput becomes A + f × B. The lognormal mu and sigma are separately weighted by resolved sample count (or 1 when absent), rather than refitted from pooled observations. Scenario utilization is clamp(combined WIP / max(1, 2 × combined developers), 0.3, 0.92); this differs from the main roster-stream load model.

Internal dependency edges disappear; external duplicates combine. For merged teams with less than two hours of overlap, half their modeled three-round-trip handoff cost remains as residual tax. Overlap with another team becomes the better original overlap. Suggested merges greedily compare improvements, normally for three rounds with a 12-developer cap and at least two recorded coupling links. Validate ownership, skills and coordination before treating a suggestion as an organizational proposal.

22. Measure: Data Quality

Use Data Quality to find missing evidence before accepting a forecast. The selected snapshot supplies team associations, sizing, assignment, stale WIP and dependency-link coverage. Whole-snapshot counts and a displayed issue sample can have different denominators; use each label's scope.

How the forecast is calculated

The UI runs 10,000 trials using a repeatable random seed. Each task samples a lognormal duration exp(mu + sigma × normal sample) from its team's model, then multiplies by size: S = 0.5, M = 1, L = 2, XL = 4. Epic import may infer sizes relative to the team's median points (thresholds 0.75, 1.5 and 3 times the median); without that median it uses point thresholds 2, 5 and 8. Missing points default to M. Review those guesses.

queue scale = [rho / (1 − rho)] / [baseline rho / (1 − baseline rho)]
adjusted duration = sampled duration × size × (0.15 + 0.85 × queue scale)

Utilization is capped at 0.97. The default 15% touch / 85% waiting split is a model assumption, not measured flow efficiency. Task start follows the latest predecessor finish plus modeled handoff. Each trial's finish is the latest task finish. Percentiles interpolate through the sorted trial finishes.

Handoff per round trip is 0.25 day for at least 4 hours of overlap, 0.5 for at least 2, 1 for positive overlap below 2, and 1.5 for zero overlap; the default is three round trips. Imported overlap currently defaults to 8 hours within a team, 6 for matching known locations and 2 otherwise. Those are location proxies; they are not Plan's date-aware site calendars.

24. Measure: Flow Actions

Use Flow Actions to investigate which active work to finish, pause or stop. The queue/dependency ranking suggests candidates for attention. An issue appearing on a freeze list is a prompt to review ownership and consequences; it is not an instruction to stop necessary work.

daily throughput = weekly throughput / 5
current modeled flow days = WIP / daily throughput
projected flow days = min(WIP, target WIP) / daily throughput
items to freeze = max(0, WIP − target WIP)

Example: WIP 10, throughput 5 items/week and target WIP 6 give one item/day, 10 current modeled days, 6 projected days and 4 items to review for freezing. This applies Little's-law-style average arithmetic with constant throughput and consistent item units. It is not a prediction that removing four items immediately makes every task finish four days earlier.

Zero or missing throughput cannot support the projection. If WIP counts epics while throughput counts non-epic completions, the units do not match. Use leaf-level WIP and inspect the retained throughput sample before using this comparison. Conway's exploratory controls do not pause Jira work; carry an agreed action into the source workflow and review the result in a new snapshot.

These views share the idea of protection relative to progress, but use different inputs and thresholds. Read the view and source before interpreting a color. The equations below are Conway's implementations, inspired by buffer management; they are not prescribed formulas from the books.

26. Learn: facilitate a Flow Game

Use Learn → Run games to practise choices under constraints. A game is a simulated world, even when initialized from a plan or dated snapshot. Its scores are learning feedback, not measured delivery forecasts or individual performance ratings.

  1. Choose a scenario: Balanced for ordinary tradeoffs, Constrained for bottlenecks and WIP, or Crisis for recovery under pressure. A saved snapshot or editable template can provide a familiar structure.
  2. Create teams and share the generated join links with participants.
  3. Explain the units: action points are a move budget; the timer is meeting time in seconds; one round represents a simulated quarter. Unused action points do not carry over.
  4. Teams apply moves and resolve rounds. Applied moves are committed for that round. Read each card's cost and rules before applying it.
  5. Allow introduction and debrief time outside the timers. The year-long epilogue is simulated; it is not an extra year of meeting time.

Debrief through the book-inspired principles: What constrained flow? What happened when we started less? Which prerequisite should have been ready? Did a local improvement help delivery? What real experiment will we try, and what evidence would change our minds?

The game blends value, trust, readiness, team health, innovation and longer-term effects. Its scoring and event rules are intentionally separate from the planning scheduler and Measure's probability model. Do not transfer game percentages into staffing or delivery promises.

28. Model boundaries and responsible decisions

Conway supports a manager's judgment by making assumptions inspectable. A finite schedule establishes a placement under supplied constraints; a statistical forecast establishes a range under supplied distributions; a snapshot establishes what was captured. None establishes complete scope, actual readiness or future performance by itself.

  • Before agreeing: reconcile import coverage, unknown effort and operating assumptions.
  • Before comparing: check agreement freshness, missing placements and calendar origins.
  • Before forecasting: check history and counting units, then simulator date conversion and resource limits.
  • Before reporting execution: check binding coverage and inferred timestamps.

Use a baseline to preserve the agreement, a scenario to explore a choice, a dated snapshot to observe the result and a decision record to retain the reason. Revisit the constraint as conditions change. The aim is better delivery decisions and learning, not a permanently green dashboard.
