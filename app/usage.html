Using Conway to plan a period

Conway turns a roster and a list of initiatives into a capacity-honest execution order — then lets you shape it, freeze it, and measure reality against it later. This guide walks the loop.

The planning ritual

  1. Plan setup — attach a roster (frozen copy of team composition), upload the initiatives matrix, set the period start, horizon and capacity loss. All of it lives under Plan setup at the top of the plan.
  2. Choose your models — work-in-progress model (strict / drum-gated / off), estimate model (effort ÷ lanes vs wall-clock), optional splitting tax and chunk size. The Set up this plan card offers recommended defaults.
  3. Review the order — the Order view shows the schedule: the verdict banner first ("N of M dates at risk"), then the ranked table. Your stated order is the working plan.
  4. Shape it — pin priorities (pin/unpin), edit sequencing attributes (✎), drag bars on the timeline, or press ⚡ Optimize order and let the rules suggest a better sequence. Accepting is always your call.
  5. Freeze it — save the agreed order as a baseline. It stores the schedule and the inputs that produced it. Compare any two baselines later.
  6. Watch the timeline — by initiative or by pod, full-screen if you need the room, filtered to one initiative or one pod.

Reading the Order view

The verdict banner

The first sentence is the whole answer: "N of M dates at risk — worst: <name>" or "every date holds". Everything below is detail.

Columns

ColumnMeaning
#the order's rank. ↳N beside it is the engine's suggestion when your order is in force.

Start / Commitraw scheduled finish, plus a buffer tail (25% of the chain by default), landing on the commit week — the week you may promise.

Bindswhat set the start: dependency, pod capacity, WIP limit, freeze, kit gate, drum stagger, or a pin you placed.

The fever chart and buffer burn

Each dot is a dated initiative read at its target date: how much of the chain should be done (x) against how much buffer the date eats (y). Zones match the Observe fever chart — under the green line is comfortable, amber is burning fast, red has spent it. Split-slice phases render as the bar's per-phase widths on each track.

Baselines and comparison

Save as baseline (Order header) freezes the current order with the inputs that produced it. Later, compare the live order against a baseline (compare) or compare two baselines directly (vs…): per-initiative rank, start and commit deltas, additions and removals. The active baseline is what actuals are measured against once Jira actuals land.

Timeline interactions

  • Drag a bar (by-pod lens) — pins its start week; the engine reschedules everything and snaps past freezes or busy lanes, naming the constraint if the pin is illegal.
  • Drag vertically — moves the slice between the pod's tracks; drops that overlap other work are refused with the conflict named.
  • ⛶ Full screen — lane-accurate dragging needs the room; ESC exits.
  • Filter — type part of an initiative (by-pod lens) or a pod (by-initiative lens); matching bars render, the rest are hidden. Waterfall order under a filter shows the chain top-to-bottom. Check hide empty pods to drop untouched pods, or show other work as ghosts to keep the context faintly visible.
  • Hover a week number — the calendar date. Hover a bar — start, finish, slack and its lane ladder if split.

Common warnings

WarningWhat to do
"pods referenced by initiatives but missing from the roster" a team name in the matrix isn't in the roster. Switch roster, or add the pod. Until then those slices cannot be scheduled.
"no period start"target dates need week 0. Set it in ⚙ Assumptions — every verdict on the page comes alive.
"beyond-horizon"the initiative cannot even start inside the period under the current order. Shorten its chain, add capacity at its constraint, or accept it for a later period.
"structurally-infeasible"the chain + buffer cannot meet the date however work is ordered. Only a later date, less scope or an earlier start moves it.

The thinking, briefly

Ordering under shared capacity with calendars, dependencies and WIP gates is non-linear — there is no single right answer, and Conway will not pretend otherwise. The engine runs several dispatch rules and offers its best as a suggestion; your order is the plan until you accept one. Constraints are protected (the drum, WIP limits, freezes); the estimates and the buffer carry the uncertainty. When reality diverges, the divergence is the finding.
